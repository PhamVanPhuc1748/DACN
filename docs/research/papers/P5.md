# P5 — SkiNet: A deep learning framework for skin lesion diagnosis with uncertainty estimation and explainability

**Citation:** Rajeev Kumar Singh, Rohan Gorantla, Sai Giridhar Rao Allada, Pratap Narra. *PLOS ONE* 17(10) (2022), e0276836. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0276836](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0276836). [Article / full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0276836). Peer reviewed: yes. **Access:** open access under CC BY. **Status: Verified (full text accessible).**

## Study and method

SkiNet combines lesion segmentation, classification, uncertainty estimation, and saliency explanations for human skin-lesion imagery (ISIC 2018). The proposed segmentation component is Bayesian MultiResUNet; the classification component uses Bayesian DenseNet-169 with dropout. The authors use test-time augmentation and dropout sampling to estimate uncertainty, and compare explanation methods including XRAI and Grad-CAM variants.

| Detail | Evidence |

## Relevance to this project

- **Category:** `related_work`, `deployment_inspiration` for an uncertain/review state and post-hoc visual explanation.
- **Applicable:** do not force every input into a definitive disease label; mark low-quality or uncertain inputs for human review.
- **Not transferable:** Softmax alone is not calibrated uncertainty; the paper's Bayesian segmentation/classification framework is not justified for the current project without substantial data and compute.
- **Cost:** the product can begin with conservative confidence/quality thresholds validated on held-out local data. Full SkiNet reimplementation is out of MVP scope.

Evidence locations: Methods and uncertainty sections; classification comparison (Tables 2–4); framework results (Tables 7–10). Confidence is high for its components and target domain, not for applicability to pigs.
